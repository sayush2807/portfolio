About Me

Making model failure
measurable

I'm an applied AI engineer working on LLM systems: designing benchmarks and the rubrics that decide what actually counts as a correct answer, building retrieval and fine-tuning around them, and doing the failure analysis that turns a score into a fix.

That loop runs in production at LexisNexis, where I benchmarked Claude Sonnet against Haiku on quality, cost, and latency, read the failure cases, and introduced negative-example prompting that moved task accuracy from 75% to 91%. A legal-summarization workflow I shipped went from a 94% to 100% SME pass rate the same way: look at what broke, then change one thing.

The instinct for interrogating data came from four years of data engineering at Axtria, moving 20M+ records per refresh across 8+ vendor feeds and learning not to trust a number until I know exactly how it was produced.
